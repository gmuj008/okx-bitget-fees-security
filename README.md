## Which exchange is cheaper: OKX or Bitget?

For ordinary spot trading, the difference is small at the standard tier.

OKX’s published fee example uses a **0.08% maker fee and 0.10% taker fee** for spot or margin trading. Bitget lists **0.10% for both maker and taker orders** at its standard spot tier. Bitget also says that paying spot fees with BGB can reduce the standard rate to 0.08%, before any further VIP discounts.

That gives OKX a modest advantage for a regular user who places maker orders and does not want to hold an exchange token to reduce fees. For market orders, the standard taker rate in the cited examples is the same: 0.10%.

The difference becomes more noticeable for frequent futures traders. OKX’s published example lists **0.02% maker and 0.05% taker** fees for futures. Bitget lists **0.02% maker and 0.06% taker**. At low trading volume, the one-basis-point difference may not change your decision. For high-frequency strategies, however, it can accumulate quickly.

### Simple futures fee example

Suppose you open and close a position with a total traded notional of 100,000 USDT.

- At a 0.05% taker fee, the trading cost would be about 50 USDT.
- At a 0.06% taker fee, the trading cost would be about 60 USDT.
- The difference is 10 USDT before funding, spread, slippage and withdrawal costs.

That calculation only compares the displayed trading commission. Futures users also need to consider:

- Funding payments
- Liquidation fees
- Spread and slippage
- Contract-specific fee rates
- VIP discounts
- Whether the order is actually filled as maker or taker
- Withdrawal and network fees

OKX explains that futures can involve funding fees, liquidation fees and expiry settlement fees in addition to ordinary maker and taker charges. Bitget also separates trading fees, funding fees, margin interest and network-based withdrawal costs.

**Verdict on fees:** OKX has a slight edge in the standard examples, especially for futures takers. Bitget can narrow the gap through BGB discounts and VIP tiers. If your trading volume is modest, execution quality and product availability may matter more than a 0.01% difference.

## Copy trading: Bitget has the clearer identity

Bitget has built much of its product positioning around copy trading. Users can browse lead traders, review performance statistics and copy spot or futures strategies. Bitget’s official guides describe features such as elite trader portfolios, copier data, profit-sharing summaries and controls for how copied positions are sized.

Bitget also uses a profit-sharing system. Its current fee guide says copy-trading followers may pay a profit share, typically between 10% and 20% of realized profit, depending on the lead trader’s settings. Standard trading fees and, for futures, funding fees still apply.

OKX also offers copy trading. Its spot copy-trading tools allow users to choose fixed or proportional copy amounts, set maximum total investment, define stop-loss controls and review lead-trader statistics. However, availability is more restricted in certain jurisdictions. OKX’s own FAQ lists the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Singapore and several other regions among locations where copy trading is not supported.

The practical difference is positioning:

- **Bitget feels more copy-trading-first.** The trader discovery process, rankings and profit-sharing mechanics are central to the experience.
- **OKX treats copy trading as one feature inside a larger exchange ecosystem.** It sits alongside bots, derivatives, options and Web3 tools.

Copy trading is not a passive-income shortcut. A lead trader’s historical return can hide leverage, drawdown, concentration and liquidation risk. Check the trader’s maximum drawdown, trading frequency, average holding time and risk settings before copying. A green percentage on a leaderboard is not a risk assessment.

**Verdict on copy trading:** Bitget is the stronger choice for users who specifically want to follow traders or strategies. OKX is reasonable if copy trading is only one part of a broader trading setup and the feature is available in your region.

## Security and proof of reserves

Security is where the current comparison needs more nuance than a simple “which exchange is safe?” ranking.

### OKX

OKX publishes proof-of-reserves information using a zk-STARK verification system. Its published reports include reserve and liability files, wallet addresses and verification tools. The September 2026 report page states that OKX held primary assets exceeding customer account assets for the listed assets, including BTC, ETH, USDT, USDC, XRP, DOGE, SOL and OKB.

Proof of reserves is useful because it provides a snapshot of assets and liabilities that users can inspect. It does not eliminate exchange, custody, regulatory, smart-contract or operational risk. It also does not mean every product is available in every country.

### Bitget

Bitget says it has published monthly proof-of-reserves reports since December 2022. Its September 2026 update reported a total reserve ratio of 135% and expanded coverage to 19 assets. A later September 30 update said user assets remained fully backed 1:1 after the security incident.

Bitget also disclosed the September 24 hot-wallet incident. According to its notice, approximately $351.6 million was affected, while cold wallets remained secure. Bitget said its user protection fund held more than $464 million and that user account balances were not affected. Independent investigations by Mandiant and SlowMist were later referenced in Bitget’s September 30 update.

The appropriate conclusion is not that Bitget is automatically unusable. It is that the incident is a material factor for anyone comparing custody risk on October 6, 2026. Users who choose Bitget may reasonably want to keep less capital on the exchange, test withdrawals with small amounts and follow future incident and audit updates.

For either exchange, sensible custody habits still matter:

1. Enable an authenticator-based two-factor method.
2. Use a unique password and protect your email account.
3. Set withdrawal address allowlists where available.
4. Keep long-term holdings in a self-custodial wallet or other arrangement appropriate for your risk tolerance.
5. Test a small deposit and withdrawal before moving a larger amount.
6. Review the exact legal entity and products available in your country.

## OKX Wallet vs Bitget Wallet

OKX has a self-custodial Web3 Wallet that supports DApps, DEX access, swaps and multiple blockchain networks. OKX states that the wallet is non-custodial, meaning users control their private keys and the platform does not take custody of assets held through the wallet.

Bitget also operates Bitget Wallet, alongside its centralized exchange and on-chain services. The useful question is not which wallet has a larger feature list. Ask which ecosystem you actually use:

- Choose OKX Wallet if you already trade on OKX and want one interface for exchange, on-chain swaps, DApps and portfolio tools.
- Choose Bitget Wallet if you use Bitget’s copy trading, bot and exchange products and want the wallet to sit inside that ecosystem.
- Use neither wallet as a replacement for understanding seed-phrase security, network selection or smart-contract risk.

A self-custodial wallet changes the risk model. The exchange no longer controls the funds, but the user becomes responsible for the recovery phrase and transaction approvals. A wallet can reduce exchange-custody exposure while increasing the consequences of a lost seed phrase or malicious DApp interaction.

- Whether registration is accepted in your country
- Which legal entity operates the account
